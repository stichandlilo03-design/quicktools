How to Calculate Your GPA: Complete Guide for University Students

Student Guide • 5 min read • Updated 2025

Your Grade Point Average (GPA) is one of the most important numbers in your academic life. It determines scholarship eligibility, honors classifications, and even your first job opportunities after graduation. But many students don't actually know how it's calculated.

How GPA Is Calculated

GPA uses a simple formula: multiply each course's grade points by its credit hours, add them all up, then divide by total credit hours.

The standard grade point scale used by most universities:

Letter GradeGrade Points
A4.0
A-3.7
B+3.3
B3.0
B-2.7
C+2.3
C2.0
D1.0
F0.0

Example Calculation

Let's say you take 4 courses this semester:

  • Math (4 credits) — Grade: A (4.0) → 4 × 4.0 = 16.0
  • English (3 credits) — Grade: B+ (3.3) → 3 × 3.3 = 9.9
  • Science (3 credits) — Grade: B (3.0) → 3 × 3.0 = 9.0
  • History (2 credits) — Grade: A- (3.7) → 2 × 3.7 = 7.4

Total quality points: 16.0 + 9.9 + 9.0 + 7.4 = 42.3
Total credits: 4 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 12
GPA: 42.3 ÷ 12 = 3.53

What GPA Do You Need?

  • 3.9+ Summa Cum Laude — Highest honors
  • 3.7+ Magna Cum Laude — High honors
  • 3.5+ Cum Laude — Honors
  • 3.0+ Dean's List — Good standing
  • 2.0+ Passing — Minimum to graduate
  • Below 2.0 — Academic probation risk

Tips to Improve Your GPA

  1. Prioritize high-credit courses — an A in a 4-credit course affects GPA more than a 2-credit one
  2. Use the Pomodoro technique — study in focused 25-minute bursts with 5-minute breaks
  3. Don't skip easy classes — they're easy A's that boost your GPA
  4. Retake failed courses — most universities replace the old grade

BMI Calculator: What Your Body Mass Index Really Means

Health Guide • 4 min read • Updated 2025

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple screening tool that estimates whether your weight is in a healthy range for your height. While it's not perfect, it's the most widely used measure by doctors worldwide.

BMI Categories

BMI RangeCategoryHealth Risk
Below 18.5UnderweightNutritional deficiency risk
18.5 – 24.9NormalLowest health risk
25.0 – 29.9OverweightModerate risk increase
30.0+ObeseHigher health risks

The BMI Formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

For example, if you weigh 70 kg and are 1.70 m tall: BMI = 70 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 70 ÷ 2.89 = 24.2 (Normal weight).

⚖️ Free BMI Calculator
→ Calculate Your BMI Now (Metric & Imperial)

Instant results with health classification. Supports both kg/cm and lbs/ft.

Important: BMI Limitations

BMI doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat. Athletes with high muscle mass may show as "overweight" despite being very healthy. BMI also doesn't account for age, gender, or body composition. It's a useful starting point, not a complete health assessment. Always consult a healthcare professional for personalized advice.
